## Overview

Use the workshop methods from Lesson 04 to move from an initial idea to a clearer product
proposition. Then build a focused prototype that makes the most important part of that proposition
tangible.

You do not need a full workshop group. Run one or two methods with available participants, or adapt
them as structured individual exercises. Clearly label assumptions when direct stakeholder or user
input is unavailable.

## Objective

1. Use one or two taught workshop methods to develop the concept.
2. Define a clear product proposition from the resulting artefacts.
3. Choose the smallest useful slice of the concept to prototype.
4. Build a rapid prototype that communicates that slice.

## Deliverable

| Section | What to include |
|---------|-----------------|
| Workshop artefacts | Outputs from one or two Lesson 04 methods, with a note explaining how each was run |
| Product proposition | What the product is, who it serves, and the value it creates |
| Prototype choice | The question or priority the prototype makes tangible and why this slice was selected |
| Prototype | A rapid prototype built from the workshop outputs |
| Reflection | What became clearer or changed through the workshop and prototyping work |

Suitable workshop methods include Future Spectrums, Jobs to Be Done, Human Moat, Impact/Effort
Prioritisation, or Product Definition. Choose methods that help you make a decision rather than
including artefacts for decoration.

## Steps

### Step 1: Choose and run the workshop methods

* Select one or two methods that address a live question in your concept.
* Run them with available participants or adapt them for individual use.
* Record the outputs, assumptions, and decisions produced.

### Step 2: Define the product proposition

* State what the product is and who it serves.
* Explain the value it creates for the target user.
* Show how the workshop artefacts informed this definition.

### Step 3: Build the prototype

* Choose a proof of concept, vertical slice, or single feature deliberately.
* Use the lowest fidelity that communicates the idea clearly.
* Build the prototype from the decisions captured in the workshop artefacts.
