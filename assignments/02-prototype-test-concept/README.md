## Overview

You've built a rapid prototype of your concept (Lesson 5) from your workshop design artefacts. Now
you put it in front of users with intent (Lesson 7). This is the proof your idea holds up when
someone actually uses it, in something close to the real world — not just clicking around a screen.

Create a clear product proposition, define system behaviour, and outline key data inputs and
outputs.

## What to submit

| Deliverable | What to include |
|-------------|-----------------|
| **The prototype** | A rapid prototype built from your workshop artefacts (Figma / link). |
| **A test plan** | The *question* you're testing and the *method* you chose (task-based · think-aloud · failure-injection · cognitive walkthrough), and who you tested with. |
| **Findings** | What actually happened, with evidence: usability, behaviour, overreliance, and trust. |
| **Next iteration** | What the testing tells you to change (direction, not "it felt fine"). |

## The bar

Intentional testing with direction: you chose a method to answer a question, not arbitrary
clicking.

## Steps

### Step 1 · Build the prototype

- Turn your workshop design artefacts into a rapid, testable prototype.
- Make a clear product proposition — what the product is and the value it creates.
- Define system behaviour and the key data inputs and outputs.

### Step 2 · Plan the test

- Decide the *question* you're testing to learn.
- Choose a method: task-based · think-aloud · failure-injection · cognitive walkthrough.
- Decide who you'll test with, and where.

### Step 3 · Test, find, and iterate

- Run the test and capture what actually happened, with evidence.
- Look beyond usability: watch behaviour, overreliance, and whether trust is calibrated.
- State the next iteration — the specific change your testing points to.
