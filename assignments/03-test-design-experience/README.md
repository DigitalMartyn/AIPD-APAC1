## Overview

Put the prototype in front of users with a clear research question, then use what you learn to
design the core experience. Show the complete journey, including what happens when the AI is
uncertain or wrong.

Testing with representative users is preferred. If access is limited, use classmates, colleagues,
or a cognitive walkthrough and state the limitation.

## Objective

1. Test the prototype using a method taught in Lesson 07.
2. Capture evidence about usability, behaviour, trust, and overreliance.
3. Turn the findings into a coherent end-to-end experience flow.
4. Design the key interface states needed to make the experience understandable.

## Deliverable

| Section | What to include |
|---------|-----------------|
| Test plan | Research question, method, participants, task, and evidence to capture |
| Findings | Observed behaviour and evidence, including trust or overreliance where relevant |
| Experience flow | The core journey, decisions, branches, and user/AI interactions |
| Key screens | Wireframes or screens for the most important moments |
| Critical states | Loading, low-confidence, incorrect output, correction, error, and escalation where relevant |
| Next iteration | The specific changes supported by the findings |

## Steps

### Step 1: Plan the test

* Define the question you need the test to answer.
* Choose task-based testing, think-aloud, failure injection, or a cognitive walkthrough.
* Identify participants and prepare a realistic task.

### Step 2: Test and capture evidence

* Observe what participants do rather than relying only on what they say.
* Record verification, challenge, blind acceptance, recovery, and decision quality where relevant.
* Separate observed evidence from your interpretation.

### Step 3: Design the experience

* Map the journey from entry point to outcome.
* Show where the user acts and where the AI contributes.
* Design important normal, uncertain, and failure states.
* Apply the findings to the flow and key screens.
